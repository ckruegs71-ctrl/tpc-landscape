That was a hot one. A lot of you sent us photos this summer of brown strips along the driveway or next to a rock bed, and asked what went wrong with the fertilizer.

Nothing did. Concrete, asphalt and rock soak up heat all day and cook the grass beside them.

Those edges are always the first to wilt. Fertilizer can't fix that. Water can. More often than not, it's a spot the sprinklers aren't quite reaching.

The good news is that fall is when lawns recover. Here's what's worth doing in the next few weeks.

Before the ground freezes

Your October list

Job one
Mow a little lower each timeAim for 2 to 2.5 inches by your last cut. Shorter grass going into winter means less matted turf and less snow mold in April.
Job two
Mow your leaves, don't rake themChopped leaves feed the soil. If you can still see more than half the grass after you mow, you're fine. If the leaves are winning, pick up the extra.
Job three
Water one more timeGive the lawn a good drink before the sprinklers shut down, and keep watering trees and evergreens through October until the ground freezes.
Don't skip this one

Sprinkler blowouts are happening now

Sprinkler lines have to be blown out with compressed air before the ground freezes. Water left in the lines over winter is how pipes and heads crack.

If you don't have someone lined up, we do this. Same people, same trucks.

Two ways to get ahead of spring

Seed now, green up in spring

It sounds backwards, but one of the best times to seed is right before winter. The seed goes down once the soil is too cold to sprout, sits under the snow, and comes up on its own in spring. It works especially well along driveways and boulevards where road salt thins the grass.

Save your spot for spring aeration

If water pooled or ran off your lawn in a light rain this year, the soil is packed tight. Core aeration opens it back up so water and fertilizer get down to the roots. Spring spots fill fast, so we're taking names now.

One for the trees

Wrap young trees before the rabbits find them

Young and thin-barked trees (maples, lindens, crabapples) can split from winter sun, and rabbits and voles chew the bark once the snow piles up. A white tree wrap or plastic guard put on this fall and taken off in spring takes care of both. If you planted anything in the last two years, it's worth ten minutes.
